## Accuracy Floor by Parameter Count: Where ~3B Lands on Medical/JP-Medical Benchmarks

**What the evidence establishes.** A ~3B model sits at a directly-measured quality floor on reasoning-type medical tasks. In the only controlled in-domain size scan (Qwen2.5 3B/7B/14B/32B/72B, arXiv 2509.04534), 3B scores ~40% on MedQA vs ~62% at 7B and ~70% at 72B, and biomedical NER F1 collapses to ~0.18 vs ~0.35 at 7B -- a sharp 3B->7B jump that then plateaus. This is English medical on GPU 4-bit, the only direct 3B in-domain datapoint available.

**No Japanese-medical number exists at 3B.** JMedBench (COLING 2025, arXiv 2409.13317) states verbatim it evaluated only "LLMs with around 7~8B parameters" for compute reasons; smallest model was 7B. So every ~3B Japanese figure is interpolated between a 1B floor and 7-8B results. Even the 7-8B out-of-box ceiling is modest: Qwen2-7B scores 41.25% IgakuQA / 44.06% JMMLU-medical / 31.03% MedQA zero-shot (42.58% micro-avg), only ~49-52% few-shot -- all far below the ~75% pass line, while GPT-4 (~76-80%) and 70B JP-tuned MedSwallow (~79-82%) clear it. A 1B JP model sits near the random floor out-of-box (~11-23%, arXiv 2412.16423).

**Task type splits the risk in two.** On narrow fine-tuned discriminative tasks (classification, NER, extraction -- de-identification-adjacent), even a 1B Japanese clinical model (NCVC-slm-1, arXiv 2412.16423) beats GPT-4o and 8-9B models, topping 6/8 JMED-LLM tasks (de-id SMDIS 0.98/0.99 kappa, disease NER 0.90 F1). The SAME model stays near floor on reasoning QA (JMMLU-Med 0.26 vs 0.82; cancer-stage 0.42 vs 0.85). Fine-tuning closes the extraction gap, not the reasoning gap. This is the central design insight: **de-identification and structured extraction are viable at ~1-3B; reasoning-heavy ICD/DPC coding and open-ended generative documentation are where the small-model risk concentrates and where NO source measures any small model.**

**Levers with quantified evidence.** Domain SFT lifts a 7B model +7.7pp IgakuQA and +17.6pp JMMLU-medical (JMedLLM-v1-7B, arXiv 2409.11783v2) -- the primary gap-closing tool, but validated at 7B not 3B, and it helps extraction more than reasoning. On quantization: 4-bit is a minor risk for extraction (Llama-3.2-3B q4_k_m loses only 2.4pp MMLU, arXiv 2512.06490), but sub-13B models have the least headroom and need W8 to keep multi-step reasoning within 2% loss (arXiv 2402.18158). The small-large gap widens with difficulty (~70B cuts reasoning errors ~2x vs ~1.3x on simple tasks, OpenReview jgVqCCg5XX), so a 3B is most disadvantaged exactly on the hardest coding reasoning.

**Load-bearing caveats.** (1) The ~3B JP-medical floor is genuinely extrapolated, not measured. (2) All evidence is transformer-on-GPU; the RWKV-7/Mamba-hybrid + 4-bit GGUF + CPU stack is completely unvalidated and may quantize differently. (3) Benchmark MCQA is a weak proxy for generative documentation quality, which nobody measures at small size. (4) The NCVC 1B wins are post-fine-tuning on discriminative tasks and do not transfer to discharge summaries or op-notes.

**Takeaway: A ~3B model is near the reasoning-QA floor and cannot pass Japanese medical QA out-of-the-box, but is demonstrably strong on fine-tuned de-identification/extraction even down to 1B -- so the small-model thesis survives as a TASK-STRATIFIED bet (4-bit for extraction/de-id; SFT + RAG + structured decoding + verifiers + human-in-loop, and likely 8-bit, for the reasoning/coding path), conditional on running our own 3B JP-medical and generative benchmarks, which do not exist in the literature.**

## RAG for a ~3B Model: Can Retrieval Close the Gap to 70B?

**Scope:** whether retrieval-augmented generation lets a ~3B model match a 70B on clinical documentation, coding, and guideline-grounded tasks — and best practices for local/offline RAG.

### What the evidence says
- **RAG alone does NOT close the gap, and is often net-negative below 7B.** On extractive QA, standard-prompting RAG lowered EM at every scale under 8B (3B: -1.9pp); with *oracle* retrieval a 3B still reached only 12.8% EM on unknown questions. The bottleneck is *context utilization*, not retrieval quality (arXiv:2603.11513, non-peer-reviewed preprint, English extractive QA — extrapolation to clinical generation is a caveat).
- **The failure mode is 'irrelevant generation'** — the model ignores the retrieved passage (73% of 3B failures). Better retrievers/re-rankers close at most half the gap; the rest needs model-side conditioning (RAG-aware training) (arXiv:2603.11513).
- **Fine-tuning beats RAG at small scale.** At 4B on medical MCQA, domain FT gave +6.8pp (p<1e-4) while RAG gave no significant gain; the best non-domain+RAG setup never caught the worst domain setup (arXiv:2604.23801). Author verdict: "RAG is not a substitute for in-weights domain knowledge at this scale."
- **7-12B is the peer-reviewed optimal band** for ICD-10 coding and clinical extraction; sub-7B is notably worse and 70B adds nothing — ~3B sits below the effective threshold out-of-the-box (Lenz et al., BioData Mining 2025, arXiv:2501.12106).
- **RAG genuinely helps LARGER local models:** it lifted a local Llama-3.2-11B on a guideline consult to close ~79% of the gap to cloud (PMC12223273); LLaMA2-70B medical QA +8pp (PMC12157099) — but no cited RAG study includes a ~3B data point, and none is a non-transformer.

### Best practices for the offline pipeline (when we do use RAG)
- **Structured retrieval > raw text for coding:** encode ICD-10/DPC as a knowledge graph + distilled guideline summaries (RAG-Coding: +3-13% micro-F1, arXiv:2605.27377 — shown on large backbones only).
- **A small reranker is cheap and effective:** monoT5 (220M) ≈ a 7B reranker at ~18x speed (arXiv:2407.01219) — fits the USB stick.
- **De-identification via distillation, not RAG:** a 184M distilled encoder hit 0.89/0.88 P/R on CPU (SHIELD, arXiv:2605.03301) — favor a dedicated distilled NER de-id component.
- **Verifier / self-refinement loops + human-in-loop:** a 7B+refinement loop reached 94% clinically-acceptable summaries, 0% critical errors (PMC12891688) — sets a plausible error-budget target.

**Takeaway: RAG is NOT the primary accuracy lever for our ~3B model — standard RAG is net-negative below 7B because small models fail to condition on retrieved text; the winning path is domain-adapted/distilled WEIGHTS + structured (KG/guideline) retrieval + RAG-AWARE fine-tuning + verifier loops + human-in-loop error budgets. RAG closes most of the cloud gap only for 11B+ transformers, and NO primary evidence validates any of this for a ~3B non-transformer on Japanese clinical text.**

## Hospital-Acceptable Eval Protocol & Published Error-Rate Benchmarks (q7)

**Scope:** how to PROVE clinical-documentation accuracy to a hospital — metrics, gold-set design, human evaluation, non-inferiority framing — and what error rates peer-reviewed/local systems actually report.

### The protocol we should adopt (reusable, cited scaffolds)
- **Factuality gold set:** clinician-defined high-salience "key facts" validated by a **7-physician majority vote**; score factuality = key-fact present, hallucination = contradiction, omission = missing key fact; scale verification with a multi-LLM **"jury" majority vote** (MedFactEval/MedAgentBrief, Grolleau et al., arXiv 2509.05878). Detected 10-25% contradiction rates. *Note: the jury-as-expert-substitute non-inferiority claim was REFUTED in verification — keep humans in the loop.*
- **Human evaluation:** dual instrument = **1-7 Likert on four dimensions** (quality, readability/conciseness, factuality, completeness) PLUS a **per-error safety log** on four axes (description, error type, likelihood of harm, extent of harm) (Osborne et al., medRxiv 2025.04.03.25325204). Use validated templates: **PDSQI-9** (JAMIA 2025, Cronbach a=0.879, ICC=0.867, >80% power; academic.oup.com/jamia/article-abstract/32/6/1050) or **modified PDQI-9 + binary hallucination flag**, blinded dual board-certified raters, RWG interrater reliability (PMC12586549).
- **Device-style harm framing:** **CREOLA**'s four transferable components — error taxonomy, iterative comparison, regulatory device-derived clinical harm framework, annotation GUI (npj Digit Med, s41746-025-01670-7) — plus the **Williams/UCSF blinded-duplicate design**: 22 attending physicians, 100 encounters, dual eval, three error types, **AHRQ-adapted 0-7 harm scoring** (JAMA Intern Med 2025, PMID 40323616).
- **Statistics:** frame as a **PRE-REGISTERED non-inferiority test against physician-written notes with a pre-specified margin** on hallucination/omission/harm — NOT a superiority test. The one cited "non-inferiority" result was actually a one-sided superiority test (a non-significant result does NOT prove equivalence).

### The scoreboard (published error rates — two non-comparable regimes)
- **Controlled sentence-level (GPT-4):** ~1.47% hallucination / ~3.45% omission, but 44% of hallucinations "major" (Asgari, npj Digit Med, s41746-025-01670-7).
- **Open-ended ENCOUNTER-level:** 42% of summaries contain a hallucination, 47% omit clinically-relevant content (CARE aggregation, arXiv 2606.08969). **Report encounter-level — the sentence-level figures are 20-30x more flattering and must not be quoted as our bar.**
- **Human baseline:** commercial ambient scribe 31% vs human 20% hallucination (p=0.01, PMC12586549); frontier LLM discharge summaries make MORE errors than physicians (**2.91 vs 1.82 errors/summary**, narrative harm 0.84 vs 0.36, both p<0.001; PMID 40323616) — a GPT-4-class UPPER bound, so a ~3B plausibly does worse.

### Direct small-model signal and gap-closers
- **Phi-3 (3.8B) was the WORST of 7 open models on hallucinations** (150 unsupported + 111 contradicted facts / 100 MIMIC-IV summaries; arXiv 2504.19061) — but confounded by verbosity, a transformer (not RWKV/Mamba), n=100, GPT-4 auto-judge. A warning, not a verdict on a 3B non-transformer.
- **De-id is capability-gated:** GPT-3.5 F1 0.39 / recall 0.67 (missed ~33% PHI) vs GPT-4 F1 0.90 (Sci Rep, s41598-025-86890-3); recall/leakage is the binding constraint — argues for a dedicated distilled de-id NER, not the generative 3B.
- **Conformal safety layer:** **CARE** gives distribution-free hallucination-risk control at alpha=0.15 without retraining — holds violations at 5.7-12.8%, flags 8-24% of sentences, captures **83-95% of hallucinations** (arXiv 2606.08969). This is our residual-risk bound for the advisory + human-confirmed design, regardless of a weak base model (caveat: exchangeability assumption may break under JP-hospital distribution shift).
- **Coding is UNEVIDENCED:** every ICD/DPC accuracy claim (43.3% precision, 0-4.8% exact match, 3.85-10.90% real-world collapse) FAILED verification — no trustworthy small-model coding number exists.

**Takeaway: We now have a concrete, hospital-defensible eval blueprint — MedFactEval key-fact gold set + PDSQI-9/modified-PDQI-9 human rubric + CREOLA/AHRQ harm taxonomy, scored at ENCOUNTER level and framed as a PRE-REGISTERED non-inferiority test against the imperfect ~20%-hallucination / 1.82-errors human baseline, with a CARE-style conformal verifier flagging 8-24% of sentences to bound residual risk. But every benchmark comes from large/frontier models (smallest, Phi-3 3.8B, was worst on hallucinations), coding accuracy is entirely unevidenced (all claims refuted), and NOTHING validates a ~3B non-transformer on Japanese text — so this protocol is the instrument we must run OURSELVES to convert the small-model thesis from extrapolation into a measured go/no-go.**

## What a ~3B Non-Transformer Actually Scores on Japanese-Medical Benchmarks (q11)

**Status: no direct measurement exists — the highest-value datapoint (our own 3B GGUF-Q4 score) must still be generated in-house.** Every Japanese-medical benchmark number in the literature comes from a transformer at 1B, 7-8B, or 70B; the target ~3B size and the non-transformer architecture together have ZERO direct evidence and remain interpolated (JMedBench "only evaluate LLMs with around 7~8B", COLING 2025 [aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.395.pdf]; smallest purpose-built JP clinical LM is NCVC-slm-1 at 1B [arxiv.org/abs/2412.16423]).

### The knowledge-recall track (ICD/DPC coding, licensing-style QA) — pessimistic
- Small models fail out-of-box: 1B scores near-random (~15-23%) on IgakuQA; best out-of-box 7B (Qwen2-7B) only 41.25% zero-shot / 51.75% few-shot on IgakuQA, 44.06/51.61 on JMMLU-medical — vs GPT-4 ~77-80% and 70B medical models ~79-82% (JMedBench [arxiv.org/html/2409.13317v1]; NCVC-slm [arxiv.org/abs/2412.16423]).
- Japanese-medical SFT gives real but bounded lift (+6.65 pts on an 8B [self-reported model card, IngentaAITeam MedExamDoc-8B]; +13.9% avg / +20.4 MedQA / +17.6 JMMLU on a 7B [arxiv.org/html/2409.11783v2]; +0.339 on a 70B base [arxiv.org/pdf/2406.14882]) — yet best domain-adapted open models still cap ~52% on IgakuQA, below the ~70% acceptance gloss.
- Architecture cuts against us here: RWKV-7 2.9B scores 55.0 on 5-shot MMLU, 10.7 pts below Qwen2.5-3B (65.7) [arxiv.org/pdf/2503.14456]; pure SSMs are documented to lag transformers ~15 pts on exactly this MMLU/in-context-recall regime [arxiv.org/pdf/2406.07887]. And 4-bit hits small models harder (8-bit beats 4-bit on 12/13 benchmarks at 8B, minimal gap at 70B; 0.5B loses ~40% relative at Q4 [arxiv.org/html/2409.11055v1]). Both effects push a Q4 3B non-transformer BELOW a naive 1B->7B interpolation on knowledge tasks.

### The structured-extraction track (de-identification, template-filling, NER) — optimistic
- After SFT, a 1B model BEAT GPT-4o on 6 of 8 JMED-LLM structured/classification/NER tasks (SMDIS 0.98/0.99 vs 0.76/0.88; NRNER F1 0.88 vs 0.39) while still failing knowledge MCQA (0.19/0.39) [arxiv.org/abs/2412.16423]. Clean separation: format-bound tasks are tractable at small scale, knowledge inference is not.

### Build guidance
- Prefer a Mamba-HYBRID (minority attention layers) over a pure SSM/RWKV: an 8B Mamba-2-Hybrid beat the same-size transformer by +2.65 pts avg over 12 tasks [arxiv.org/pdf/2406.07887].
- Treat Q4 as a measured tradeoff; evaluate Q5/Q6/mixed precision for the 3B build.
- SFT is not free upside — Meditron-7B REGRESSED below base via catastrophic forgetting (21.94% vs 22.69%) and a LLaMA-3.2-3B QLoRA showed zero ROUGE-L movement (0.3052->0.3052) [aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.395.pdf; arxiv.org/html/2510.05003v1]. Recipe (JP/EN balance, epochs, replay) is decisive.
- Report TWO separate accuracy tracks, never one blended number.

**Takeaway: Anchor the product on structured/format-bound tasks (de-id, extraction, templating) where ~3B is genuinely competitive — even beating frontier models after SFT — and offload knowledge-recall tasks (ICD/DPC coding) to RAG + verifiers + human-in-loop, because no ~3B config will clear the licensing bar on knowledge MCQA by scale or SFT alone. The direct 3B benchmark still must be run.**

## Can RAG + Constrained Decoding + Verifiers Lift a 3B to an Acceptable Coding Bar? (q13) — QUALIFIED YES, BY COMPOSITION ONLY, WITH NO 3B/DPC/JP NUMBER YET

**Status: the coding task is the widest small-vs-large gap and the least-evidenced corner of the product. No direct ~3B, DPC, Japanese, or CPU coding accuracy number exists anywhere — every quantitative figure below is English, mostly 7B-70B (fine-tuning: 1B/3B/8B on synthetic/English notes). The pipeline thesis is supported in principle but the product number MUST be measured in-house.**

### The zeroth-order truth: out-of-box coding is unacceptable at ANY size
- GPT-4 (best of 6 frontier models; smallest tested 70B) agreed with a certified human coder on only **15.2% of codes (26.4% category-level), Cohen's kappa ~0.01 (chance-level)**, against a human inter-coder **kappa=0.86** over 165 codes / 50 inpatient notes ([medrxiv 2024.04.29.24306573]). Consequence: score the pipeline against a **human-adjudicated DPC gold set**, never against zero-shot LLM output, and design as advisory + human-confirmed. "No trustworthy small-model coding number exists" is an argument-from-absence, not a measurement — nothing under 70B was tested.

### Per-component contribution (the answer to "quantify each stage")
- **Fine-tuning = the dominant lever, but clean-input only.** Fine-tuned Llama-3.2-3B: **0.13% -> 98.83% exact-match** on standardized ICD-10 code-description pairs, with 1B/3B/8B all converging to ~98.8% (size irrelevant on clean pairs). BUT the SAME 3B collapses to **4.18% on multiple concurrent conditions, 67.92% on reordered expressions, ~69% top-1 / ~87% category on real MIMIC-IV notes**, where size reasserts itself (sentence integration 49.48% at 1B vs 87.99% at 8B) ([nature s44401-025-00018-3]). Read 98.8% as a ceiling, not a real-note capability.
- **RAG = large relative, low absolute.** Over a >1M-visit ED DB, RAG lifted Qwen-2-7B **0.8%->17.6%** and Gemma-2-9b-it **7.2%->26.4%** exact-match — 26.4% was the small-model ceiling ([medrxiv 2024.10.15.24315526]). RAG alone CANNOT reach an acceptable exact-match bar at 3B. Same study's caveat: its LARGE models (GPT-4/70B) were judged by physicians to *beat* provider coders, and the authors reject exact-match-vs-provider as a metric — so the gold set needs a clinical-adjudicated correctness/specificity axis, not exact-match alone.
- **Constrained/grammar decoding = large secondary lever for schema validity.** On a 223M Flan-T5, grammar-constrained decoding added **0.35-0.43 absolute F1** on schema-bound slot-filling (glaucoma 0.045->0.47), but the delta is inflated by a near-zero baseline and constrained F1 (0.29-0.47) is still sub-clinical ([frontiersin frai.2024.1406857]). "Eliminates post-hoc validation" was **refuted 0-3** — it guarantees valid code *structure*, not clinical *correctness*.
- **Verifiers = bound and surface errors, do NOT raise accuracy.** CARE/RCPS-style conformal layers give distribution-free finite-sample guarantees at tunable alpha and improved reviewers by **+28.6pp omission detection (50.4%->79.0%) with ~5% less review time** (n=3 clinicians, preliminary) ([arxiv 2606.08969; arxiv 2603.00924]).

### The two hard constraints the pipeline imposes
- **Human-in-loop cost is severe and section-dependent.** To honor alpha, conformal control forces large review fractions: FDA-label sections needed **41-100% rejection** at alpha=0.05 (Pediatric Use 100%); RadGraph at alpha=0.10 rejected 59.3% (GPT-4.1) / 19.6% (Llama-4-Maverick) ([arxiv 2603.00924]). The DPC review-flag fraction is **not one number** — measure it per DPC section on our gold set; tightening alpha can collapse throughput.
- **Composition multiplicatively decays trust.** de-id NER -> coding -> verifier at 90%/stage yields at most **(1-alpha)^K = 72.9% end-to-end (K=3)**; empirically naive independent conformal fell to **86.5%** vs **PASC 96.4%** / Bonferroni 93.4% at equal set size ([arxiv 2605.18812]). The offline pipeline needs **pipeline-aware joint calibration**, not per-stage tuning.

### Gold-set design constraints (carried forward)
- Weight for the systematic **frequent-vs-rare gap: micro-F1 0.79 frequent vs macro-F1 0.42 infrequent** (35-study PRISMA review, [medrxiv 2025.07.30.25330916]) — report the two separately with hierarchical/clinical-context weighting. "Additive component gains" was refuted 0-3; components interact, so measure jointly.
- **Quantization worst-case is our exact corner.** Larger models tolerate 4-bit better (7B -6.8% to -10% avg vs 70B ~flat); no study tests 3B. Counterweight: Q4_K_S loses only **~0.43% at 8B** (GSM8K), but format-specific and unproven at 3B on reasoning-heavy coding ([arxiv 2405.03146; arxiv 2601.14277v1]). The "bigger 4-bit beats smaller high-precision at equal memory" claim was refuted 1-2 — a live, unsettled risk. Evaluate Q5/Q6/mixed precision on the coding path specifically.

**Takeaway: The evidence supports a QUALIFIED YES that a 3B base can be lifted to a useful ADVISORY coding bar, but ONLY by composition — SFT (largest lever, clean-input only) + RAG (necessary, low absolute) + grammar constraints (schema validity) + a conformal verifier (bounds/surfaces residual error at a tunable, section-variable review cost) with PASC-style joint calibration across the de-id -> coding -> verifier chain. No component alone suffices; verifiers add safety not accuracy; the human-in-loop review fraction (20-100% in cited domains) is the real throughput constraint. Coding remains the WEAKEST link and the only major product task with ZERO transferable small-model evidence — the exact-match + clinical-adjudicated DPC accuracy of our own fine-tuned ~3B Q4 GGUF on a Japanese DPC gold set is the single irreplaceable go/no-go datapoint and must be generated in-house before this leg of the thesis can be called.**

## Does a ~3B Capture the 7B SFT Uplift, and Does QLoRA Cost Us Any of It? (q15 — RE-POSE of q2) — INFERRED YES WITH A TASK-DEPENDENT CEILING; QLoRA IS NOT THE BINDING CONSTRAINT

**Status: the exact 3B-vs-7B SFT fraction is UNMEASURED — no controlled 1B/3B/7B size-scan of SFT delta exists on JP or general medical QA, and both anchor papers explicitly disclaim extrapolation. The answer below is bounded by disjoint single-scale points (1.2B, 3.8B, 4B, 7B, 70B) across different bases, languages, and tasks — converging, but not a measurement.**

### The 7B anchor is confirmed AND was itself LoRA
- JMedLLM-v1-7B (LoRA-SFT on Qwen2-7B) gained **+7.7pp IgakuQA (44.6->52.3) and +17.6pp JMMLU-medical (33.2->50.8)** ([arxiv 2409.11783]). Decisive nuance for our pipeline: the reference uplift was achieved by **LoRA, not full-parameter SFT** — so our constrained LoRA/QLoRA build is method-matched to the exact number we target; LoRA is not a handicap relative to the reference.

### The uplift mechanism survives at 3-4B when the base is strong — but at reduced magnitude and task-dependent
- **Upper signal (~3.8B):** MedMobile (phi-3-mini) reached **75.7% MedQA-USMLE** (smallest to pass the ~60% physician bar), SFT alone contributing **+8.4pp**. CAVEAT: 75.7% needs multi-sample ensembling (multiplied CPU cost — adverse for offline llama.cpp); **single-pass SFT+CoT ~68.3%** still clears 60% ([arxiv 2410.09019]). English MCQA, not JP generation.
- **On-family signal (~4B, Q4 GGUF, llama.cpp-class):** MedGemma 4B beat Gemma-3 4B by **+6.8pp** on full MedQA (53.3 vs 46.4, McNemar p<1e-4) — domain SFT survives quantized CPU serving, at roughly **half** the 7B JMedLLM magnitude ([arxiv 2604.23801], single-author preprint).
- **Pessimistic floor (~1.2B, JP, knowledge QA):** NCVC-slm-1 stays **below 20% on IgakuQA even after SFT** (uplift ~+5-6pp) — but this is an undertrained from-scratch base, so it conflates scale with base quality. Lesson: **do not train from scratch; start from a strong modern 3B base.**

### The decisive axis is TASK TYPE, not parameter count
- The SAME 1.2B NCVC-slm-1, after SFT, **beats GPT-4o on 6 of 8 JMED-LLM extraction/NER/classification tasks** (SMDIS kappa 0.98 vs 0.76) and fails ONLY the two high-inference tasks (JMMLU-Med 0.44 vs 0.87) ([arxiv 2412.16423]). Our **de-id / NER / structured extraction map onto the favorable class; free-text knowledge reasoning and knowledge-heavy coding map onto the unfavorable class** — reinforcing the two-track architecture already in the playbook. A ~2-bits/param capacity law ([arxiv 2404.05405]) gives the mechanism: knowledge RECALL scales with params (and int4 degrades it further), while extraction needs little stored knowledge — so route knowledge to RAG, keep extraction in-model.

### QLoRA does NOT sacrifice meaningful uplift — but is unproven below 4B
- Full-FT beats LoRA by only **+3.6pp USMLE-avg (0-6.2pp)** at 70B (largest gap on reasoning-style MedMCQA); **4-bit QLoRA lands only 1-4pp below LoRA** on clinical extraction, preserving most of a 10-20pp uplift ([arxiv 2404.14779; medrxiv 2025.10.21.25338506]). CAVEAT: all evidence is 8B/70B Transformers (gentler quantization than 3B); losing 2-4pp of a 10-20pp uplift is ~15-30% of it, concentrated on reasoning. QLoRA is unlikely to bind, but verify at 3B/Q4.
- **Counter-signal at exactly 3B:** a LLaMA-3.2-3B QLoRA showed **zero ROUGE-L movement (0.3052->0.3052)** ([arxiv 2510.05003]) — confounded by 2 epochs / small data / metric saturation, but proof that **SFT uplift at 3B is not automatic**: recipe (epochs, JP data volume, sensitive metric) is decisive.

### Evidence-quality flags
- Smallest peer-reviewed-validated high-accuracy medical model is **7B (Meerkat-7B, Nature npj)**; MedMobile's 3.8B is lower-tier (BMJ Digital Health & AI); the 4B, QLoRA, and null-3B sources are single-author preprints. **No peer-reviewed work validates a 3B model** — raising the bar for our in-house validation. Two JMedLoRA scaling claims (uplift scales with size / small = lower fraction) were REFUTED in verification (cross-family confound) and must NOT be cited as clean size-scaling evidence. RAG can HURT small models (**-12.6pp at 3.8B**), so RAG must be regression-tested per task, never assumed.

**Takeaway: A ~3B model most likely captures a SUBSTANTIAL, order-comparable fraction of the 7B SFT uplift — but only (a) from a strong modern base (never from-scratch), (b) at reduced magnitude (~half the 7B delta, per the 4B on-family point), and (c) split sharply by task: extraction/NER/de-id land in the class where even 1.2B beats GPT-4o after SFT, while knowledge-reasoning QA hits a lower absolute ceiling that scale and SFT alone will not clear. The constrained QLoRA pipeline is NOT the binding constraint (costs ~15-30% of the uplift at most, and the 7B reference was itself LoRA). The irreplaceable unknowns remain: the exact 3B fraction is unmeasured in the literature, and NONE of it is tested on the product's RWKV-7/Mamba non-transformer under Q4 on CPU — so a same-recipe 3B-base SFT on IgakuQA/JMMLU-medical, plus a full-SFT-vs-LoRA-vs-Q4-QLoRA delta check at 3B, must be run in-house before this leg of the thesis is called.**

## Task-Type Split of SFT Uplift and the Labeled-Data Marginal-Return Curve (q16 — RE-POSE of q2) — CONFIRMED SPLIT; DATA CURVE EXTRAPOLATED

**Status: the extraction-wins/reasoning-lags split is CONFIRMED (high confidence) from the single sovereign JP-clinical data point (NCVC-slm-1, 1.2B); the 1k/10k/100k data-volume curve is MEDIUM confidence — its SHAPE is well-established but every number is extrapolated from English/German 8B transformers, none from a ~3B non-transformer on Japanese clinical text.**

### SFT uplift concentrates on extraction, not reasoning — quantified
- Instruction-tuning 1.2B NCVC-slm-1 lifted it from ~0.00 to the **HIGHEST score of any model, beating GPT-4o, on 6 of 8 JMED-LLM tasks** (all NER + classification/similarity), while the two reasoning tasks (JMMLU-Med, RRTNM) stayed only "comparable or below" GPT-4o ([arxiv 2412.16423]; [ANLP 2025 Q4-4]).
- Deltas are enormous and uneven: **NER 0.00->0.90 (MRNER-disease), 0.00->0.88 (NRNER)**, both beating GPT-4o's 0.54/0.39; **SMDIS classification kappa 0.01->0.98**; CRADE ~0.00->0.61; JCSTS 0.05->0.75 — but **JMMLU-Med reasoning gained only +0.13 kappa (0.13->0.26)** vs GPT-4o 0.82 ([arxiv 2412.16423]). Our de-id/NER/structured-extraction path maps onto the favorable class; knowledge-reasoning and knowledge-heavy coding map onto the unfavorable class.
- The reasoning ceiling is a size limit SFT cannot clear at ~1B: **IgakuQA licensing exam ~18-19% after SFT** (near 5-choice random) vs GPT-4 ~78%, 70B 67-80%, ~75% pass bar — a **~60pp gap**, SFT moving it only ~+5pp; authors explicitly conclude a larger model is needed ([ANLP 2025 Q4-4]). CAVEAT: confounded by NCVC's tiny ~9B-token unique corpus and no controlled size sweep — the from-scratch 1.2B base is weak; a strong modern 3B base may do better.

### The labeled-data marginal-return curve is TASK-SHAPE-DEPENDENT
- **Extraction/classification saturate fast:** Llama-3-8B reached **88% at 200 examples, 90.6% at 1,000, peaking ~93.9% at 6,500 and flat to 10k**; NER plateaued at **~300 examples**; a survey places the PEFT elbow at **10k-40k** and finds **1k-10k well-labeled examples beat 100k+ low-quality ones** ([arxiv 2407.13906v1]; [arxiv 2503.21349v1]; [arxiv 2411.09539]). The first order-of-magnitude of data buys most of the uplift; the bottleneck is label quality/coverage, not raw volume.
- **Reasoning/coding/multihop do NOT plateau:** they follow an **unsaturating power law (P proportional to D^(1/b))** where a small seed only aligns style while accuracy keeps rising with data far past 1k ([arxiv 2410.03717]); a multimodal SFT scaling law shows OCR/extraction relies heavily on SFT data while perception scales with model size ([openreview xZt6uSmL34]). Implication: closing residual reasoning at ~3B needs LARGE, not small, SFT volume with steeply diminishing returns — implausible for JP clinical coding.
- **Cross-scale corroboration of cheap extraction:** 24B Mistral+Self-Refine hit **97.8% of GPT-4.1 on Japanese PHI**; 8B Llama-3 reached 70B-parity on medical NER from **~200-300 examples** ([nature s41598-026-36904-5]; [arxiv 2503.21349v1]) — both ABOVE our 3B budget, and the 24B used CPU-costly multi-pass.

### Evidence-quality flags
- NO DIRECT ~3B, NO NON-TRANSFORMER, NO POST-Q4 evidence — the split rests on one 1.2B transformer; data-volume numbers are English/German 8B transformers extrapolated to our stack. NCVC extraction wins are partly in-distribution (SFT used JMED-LLM train split), possibly overstating held-out de-id generalization. Three claims were REFUTED and must not be cited: "encoders far more param-efficient", "model size dominates data size / small models less sample-efficient", and "win-rate overstates small-data SFT ~3x".

**Takeaway: SFT uplift at sub-3B scale is sharply and confirmedly concentrated on extraction/NER/classification — where even 1.2B beats GPT-4o after SFT and the curve saturates by ~200-1,000 (sweet spot ~6,500) examples — while reasoning/knowledge-heavy coding follows an unsaturating power law that small SFT volume cannot cheaply close (~60pp IgakuQA gap at 1.2B). This is decisive design guidance: keep extraction/de-id IN-MODEL and label only ~a few thousand high-quality JP examples per task; route knowledge/coding reasoning to RAG + structured decoding + verifiers + human-in-loop rather than trying to SFT it in. The irreplaceable unknowns are the same as q15: the exact JP-clinical curve at 3B Q4 on held-out data (esp. the coding elbow), whether Q4 erodes the near-parity extraction gains, and whether RWKV-7/Mamba share the transformer data-efficiency curves — all must be measured in-house.**

## RAG Context-Utilization on ~3B Non-Transformer Models (q17)

**Question:** Does the sub-7B "poor context-utilization / irrelevant generation" RAG failure mode transfer to RWKV-7 / Mamba-hybrid architectures at ~3B?

### What we now know
- **The evidence gap is confirmed, not closed.** There is ZERO primary evidence measuring RAG context-utilization or irrelevant-generation for RWKV-7 or Mamba-hybrid at ~3B. The study that established the sub-7B failure mode (arXiv:2603.11513) tested decoder-only transformers only; the one ~3B SSM/hybrid paper at scale (arXiv:2507.12442) reports only latency/memory/energy, no retrieval accuracy.
- **The failure mode itself is severe on transformers <7B.** Adding retrieval context destroys 42-100% of previously-known answers; "irrelevant generation" (model ignores context) dominates — 73% of oracle failures at 3B. Net effect of naive RAG at this scale can be NEGATIVE, and the bottleneck is context *utilization*, not retrieval quality (arXiv:2603.11513).
- **Architectural theory points the wrong way for pure SSMs.** Fixed-size recurrent state makes Mamba/RWKV/H3 provably weaker at recall/copying — the exact capability RAG stresses (Jelassi 2024, arXiv:2402.01032; Arora 2024, arXiv:2402.18668; formal proof in arXiv:2507.00449). So a naive pure-recurrent RAG stack is plausibly *equal or worse* than the transformer failure mode. (Caveat: these papers come from groups pushing competing architectures.)

### Three mitigations that soften this for our actual choices
1. **The deficit is partly optimization, not a hard ceiling.** RWKV/Mamba/DeltaNet *can* learn recall in a narrow LR window (Okpekpe & Orvieto 2025, arXiv:2508.19029); one attention layer inserted into a Mamba stack restores perfect copying (arXiv:2402.01032); Jamba-hybrid retrieves well (RULER 92.6) — but at 12B active/52B params where attention layers likely do the work, so this does NOT cleanly transfer to a small pure recurrent model.
2. **Naive one-pass RAG is the worst-matched pattern.** The weakness comes from ingesting the full context at once; chunk-and-summarize inference flips a 3B recurrent model from -5.68% to +3.61% vs transformers on LongBench (Smooth Reading, arXiv:2507.19353). Inference strategy is a real lever — but that method digests the *full* context iteratively (not selective retrieval) and costs latency on CPU.
3. **A buildable candidate exists.** RWKV-7 2.9B 'Goose' is Apache-2.0, GGUF-4bit-ready for llama.cpp, matches 3B transformer SoTA on English (arXiv:2503.14456) — but on *generic* multilingual benchmarks, not Japanese clinical text/de-id/coding, and by its own authors.

### Open risk for our eval protocol
Quantization (4-bit GGUF) effects on recall are unstudied in every cited source — must be ablated. Selective RAG at 3B on non-transformers remains directly unmeasured. (This ablation, plus the reasoning/coding precision sweep, is now folded into the new in-house RAG go/no-go question q24, which absorbed the dropped q14.)

**Takeaway:** RAG is NOT proven usable in our ~3B non-transformer stack — the sub-7B "model ignores retrieved context" failure is plausibly equal-or-worse for pure RWKV-7, with no direct evidence either way. Treat RAG as an unvalidated risk requiring in-house benchmarking (naive one-pass vs chunk-and-summarize, fp16 vs 4-bit) before any clinical feature depends on it; prefer an attention-hybrid and/or bake knowledge into weights via fine-tuning/distillation for the highest-stakes tasks (de-id, ICD/DPC coding) rather than trusting at-inference retrieval.


## Non-Inferiority Margin: Deriving a Pre-Registrable Target for an Advisory, Human-Confirmed Tool (q19)

**There is no off-the-shelf margin to cite** — no source documents a numeric NI margin that a Japanese hospital IRB or PMDA has accepted for an advisory, human-confirmed LLM drafting tool (IBA, Tokyo Foundation regulatory scans). We must derive and pre-register one, then negotiate it with the ethics board.

**Recommended derivation — FDA/ICH E10 fixed-fraction, applied per-endpoint by severity.** The regulatory-standard method (CMAJ Mulla 2013; PMC3113981; PMC12866653; PMC3764030) preserves a fraction of the comparator effect — conventionally 50% (explicitly "arbitrary"; 2/3 and 75% also used) — and tightens the margin for serious/irreversible harm while loosening it when the tool has a better safety profile. Applied to this product:
- **Human baseline (anchor):** 1.82 ± 1.94 total errors (inaccuracies + omissions) per physician "brief hospital course" narrative; a GPT-4-class LLM scored 2.91 ± 2.54 (P<.001) (UCSF blinded study, JAMA Intern Med 2025, medRxiv 2024.09.29.24314562). Do NOT conflate this with the "~20% hallucination" figure, which is a different metric/denominator from separate GPT-4 ED studies.
- **Loose margin on error/omission COUNTS:** ~50% preservation of 1.82 implies a starting proposal of roughly **≤0.9 extra errors/summary**.
- **Strict, binding margin on AHRQ harm:** per-error severity was statistically equal (1.35 vs 1.34, P=.99) but **aggregate encounter-level harm was significantly higher for the LLM (0.84 vs 0.36, P<.001; gap ~0.48, both <1 "low harm")**. Place the binding NI margin on aggregate AHRQ harm, not per-error harm. Severe errors (≥4) were rare (1 LLM vs 0 physician).

**Measure the margin on POST-human-confirmation output, not raw drafts.** Unedited frontier output is far above baseline (GPT-4: 42% hallucination, 47% omission, 33% error-free; GPT-3.5 worse — PLOS Digital Health). The human-confirmation step is what any NI claim rests on; the eval must include the clinician edit in-loop.

**Direct the error budget at OMISSION.** Frontier pilots show omission (25%) > inaccuracy (20%) > hallucination (2%), with 88% of unedited summaries "no harm" (MedAgentBrief PMC13156793; PMC11023681) — but these are frontier, single-pilot, and the 2% hallucination figure was explicitly refuted as generalizing to 3B.

**The margin question is entangled with an unproven small-model accuracy question — this is the make-or-break caveat.** Every favorable benchmark (structured pipeline 1.47% hallucination / 3.45% omission, npj Digit Med CREOLA; 88% no-harm; sub-human major-error rates) used GPT-4/Gemini-2.5-class models. The ONLY ~3B data point, Phi-3 3.8B, produced the MOST hallucinations of any model tested (arXiv 2504.19061) — negative on exactly the failure mode that matters, and it is a transformer, only a proxy for our RWKV/Mamba. Gap-closing levers work in principle (CREOLA; LoRA lifting Llama3-8B from 32.7%→91.3% on 398 examples, arXiv 2503.21349) but are unproven for a 3B non-transformer on generation/coding, and none of the cited evidence is Japanese-language or ICD/DPC coding.

**Takeaway: Pre-register a two-tier fixed-fraction margin — a loose count margin (~≤0.9 extra errors/summary) and a strict, binding equivalence margin on aggregate AHRQ harm (target within ~0.48 of the 0.36 physician score), tested on human-confirmed output via a formal TOST — but the margin is only meaningful once we have measured our own 3B model's post-confirmation error/harm rate on Japanese text; that measurement, not the margin math, is the gating experiment.**
